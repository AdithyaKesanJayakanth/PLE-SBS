# PLE-SBS in one minute

**Video script.** First person, in Adithya's voice. One continuous narration over eight scenes.

- Runtime 56 s · 1920×1080 · 30 fps · narration 194 words
- Narrator: ElevenLabs "Eric (calm, composed American male)", eleven_multilingual_v2, speed 1.2
- Built by `video/make_video.py`; this file is written by the same run, so the times are the ones in the video.

## Logline

A student who once designed roller-coaster tracks around what a human body can take asks how a launch crew gets down from a fuelled rocket, builds a validated simulator to find out, breaks his own first design, and fixes it with a switch yard: one wire, and a braked bay for every pod.

## Narration only (for recording)

1. Last year I designed roller-coaster tracks around the limits of the human body. The thrill and the limit turned out to be the same number.
2. Then a visit to ISRO raised a different question. A crew works a hundred metres up a tower, beside a fuelled rocket. If the alarm sounds, how do they get down?
3. NASA's slidewire baskets are each built for one pad. I wanted a method for any rocket, on any tower.
4. So I turned propellant into TNT equivalent to size the safe distance, modelled the sagging wire, and built a simulator. Untuned, it matched the Shuttle basket's speed within three percent.
5. Then I ran it until it broke. Copying Artemis onto a low pad failed ninety-three percent of runs. Geometry fixed it, and gave a sizing rule for any pad.
6. My add-on is a switch yard. Self-indexing points send each pod, from one shared wire, into its own braked bay. Eight seconds between pods, one point four g with a failed brake, and no power.
7. What I learned: design a safety system at its corners. Once the hardware works, the seconds left are human. I'm Adithya, and this is PLE-SBS.

## Notes

- Every pod position in scenes 5 and 7 comes from the model in `sim/core.js` (design D5, generic 42 m pad, nine people). The capsule-exit time is set to zero so all three pods are seen in sequence; with the paper's 60 s exit the third pod leaves at 90 s.
- The figures are the paper's own (10 June 2026). The roller-coaster track in scene 2 is a schematic drawn for the video, not a figure from the earlier paper.
- The launcher and pad are generic. The visit to ISRO is named only as the inspiration; no ISRO pad, vehicle or person is referred to.
- Rebuild: `.venv/bin/python video/make_video.py` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set (clips are cached, so a rebuild does not re-bill). `--quick` renders a 10 fps preview; `--script-only` rewrites this file from the cached narration without rendering.
